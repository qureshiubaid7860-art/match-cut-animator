How the match cut works

A conventional match cut links two shots through a shared shape or movement. In this text version, the repeated phrase provides the visual link: its screen position stays fixed while the surrounding article changes at a measured rhythm.

For each candidate page, MATCH CUT checks the target text and its location, then aligns the match to a common center point. A direct page cut keeps the sequence readable. The highlight and restrained sound mark each new article without adding a camera move.

Use a phrase that can be found

Names, places, and topic terms can make a strong focal point. Very common words may appear in many sources but can be harder to distinguish in body text. A specific two- or three-word phrase can provide a clearer editorial thread.

Before sharing, preview the video and open the source notes. Those notes identify public article excerpts and mark locally generated fictional pages.
